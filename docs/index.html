About the workshop

Reliable advice on farming, health, schooling, power and the environment already exists, but much of it sits in English documents that are hard to search or question. In this workshop, mixed teams of staff and students learn how language AI works by building a small assistant that answers everyday questions from trusted local sources, in Igbo or English, and always shows where its answer came from.

Learn by building

Every day starts with a short talk, then two hands-on labs. By Friday your team has a working prototype.

No coding needed

Guided notebooks run in your web browser. Each lab has a simple path for beginners and a stretch path for those with some programming.

Built for here

Real questions from Anambra communities, in Igbo and English, with a strong focus on using AI responsibly.

One project, five steps

Each team builds a bilingual community knowledge assistant, adding one piece each day.

  1. Monday

    1. Gather

    Collect real community questions and trusted answers in English and Igbo.

  2. Tuesday

    2. Understand

    Teach the computer to sort questions by topic.

  3. Wednesday

    3. Extract

    Pull out key facts and build an English and Igbo glossary.

  4. Thursday

    4. Answer

    Find the best answer and reply with its source.

  5. Friday

    5. Test and showcase

    Check it honestly, present it, and compete for awards.

How to apply

Applications are open to academic and non-academic staff, postgraduate and undergraduate students of Peter University and nearby institutions. The form takes about 10 minutes.

  1. Complete the Expression of Interest form by Sunday 8 November 2026, 23:59 WAT.
  2. Two reviewers score each application without seeing names or institutions.
  3. We select 50 participants, balancing staff and students, women and men, disciplines and institutions.
  4. Everyone who applies hears from us by Monday 16 November 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Every lab has a guided path for beginners. Teams mix people with and without programming experience.

Does it cost anything?

No. The workshop, lunch, refreshments and materials are free, funded by the Friends of Lancaster University in America.

Do I need my own laptop?

It helps but is not required. The Peter University computer laboratory is available, and teams share devices.

Is travel or accommodation covered?

No. Participants arrange their own daily travel to Peter University.

Do I need to attend all five days?

Yes. The project builds day by day, and certificates go to participants who attend all five days.

Do I need to speak Igbo?

No, but Igbo speakers and writers are especially welcome. Every team needs some.

What should I prepare?

A Google account, which lets you open the lab notebooks. We will send joining instructions to selected participants on Monday 23 November.

Who can I contact?

Email Dr Ignatius Ezeani at i.ezeani@lancaster.ac.uk.
